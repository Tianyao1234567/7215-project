I was responsible for the original YOLOv8n baseline for Topic 3 and for preparing the model interface required by members 06 and 07. My contribution covered the data acceptance checks, the training workflow, retention of experimental evidence, and baseline material for member 08. I used AI assistance to prepare and review code and documentation, and I have separated that assistance and the reused open-source framework from the project-specific work.

For data acceptance, I organised the revised delivery around its 1,761 per-image JSON annotations. The previous TXT locations could not serve as the current ground truth because one retained the old annotations and the other contained incomplete exports. The project scripts rebuilt the normalized YOLO rectangles and checked class IDs, image dimensions, coordinate bounds, source image hashes, and frozen label hashes. The audit and ten-image visual sample are documented in reports/new_json_20261105. These checks support the reported scope of acceptance without claiming that every annotation received a complete manual review.

I retained the existing 1,371/171/171 split rather than generating a new random split. I preserved all original JSON and isolated 48 conflicting or related images in datasets/quarantine. This maintained the exclusion policy while keeping the evidence available for member 03 to resolve. I also documented that inferred filename families do not establish independent printing-process groups.

For the baseline experiment, I prepared train_baseline.py, evaluate_baseline.py, predict_baseline.py, and the shared project runtime configuration. A real-data resource check verified training, checkpoint saving, reloading, validation, and prediction before the formal run. The formal experiment, yolov8n_newjson_from_official_20261105_213549, started from official COCO weights and completed all 100 epochs with batch size 16 and 640-pixel inputs. Its results.csv, complete terminal log, configuration, environment details, and separate best.pt and last.pt are retained. The saved best checkpoint was then reloaded for validation on 171 images. Its mAP50 was 95.47% and mAP50-95 was 60.03%.

I prepared a handoff package with class mappings, frozen split manifests, independent evaluation and prediction commands, training curves, validation plots, and representative outputs. The error examples identify unmatched predictions and annotations at a fixed confidence threshold rather than presenting selected images as a substitute for evaluation. I also organised the D-drive project, preserved the reference models and historical run, and recorded each removed redundant dataset or archive. This released approximately 25.49 GB of disk space during cleanup.

My baseline provides a reproducible reference for the lightweight model comparison. Member 05 remains responsible for the lightweight detector, member 06 for unified accuracy and speed comparisons, and member 07 for the demonstration. The frozen test set remains available to that evaluation workflow. These deliverables allow the team to reproduce the baseline and compare it under a stated protocol.
